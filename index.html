1. About this policy and contact

CT Audience Manager is an independent, free Chrome extension developed and operated by an individual, publicly known as Iggy (the “operator”, “we”, or “us”). This policy explains how the extension and its optional Discovery directory handle information. It also describes how information is handled by independent services that the product uses.

For privacy inquiries, requests concerning a profile, access or deletion requests, or support, email UkrainianVapeMasters@gmail.com. Public X profile: https://x.com/UkrainianVapeM1. Do not send passwords, session cookies, access tokens, or unnecessary sensitive information.

The extension and the optional directory are distinct processing environments. Independent platforms and service providers are responsible for their own services and policies.

2. Information processed in your browser

Depending on the tools you use, CT Audience Manager processes X account IDs and handles, display names, avatar URLs, followers and following, public account counters, available public activity observations, and optional score observations. It also stores your filters, preferences, notes, favourites, snapshots, collection progress, technical diagnostics, and supported export or backup data.

The primary storage is local Chrome extension storage. Your full audience lists, personal notes, favourites and snapshots are not uploaded to our Discovery database. Files you export or back up remain under your control. The personal backup feature covers supported personal settings, notes, and favourites; it is not a complete copy of every audience record or snapshot.

Local exports and sharing. Exported audience files are generated locally and are not automatically uploaded to our servers or published by CT Audience Manager. You decide whether to share, publish, sell, or otherwise distribute an exported file. We do not control subsequent user-initiated distribution of that file; this does not change our responsibility for processing that CT Audience Manager itself performs.

When you start X audience collection, the extension makes automated read requests using your existing signed-in X browser session. To do this, it accesses session-related information, including an X security cookie, and observes selected X request URLs and limited operation metadata. It does not collect passwords you type or read X private messages. It does not track general browsing across unrelated websites.

Collection may be interrupted, incomplete, or restricted by X. Saved tasks can resume only in accordance with your current collection choices; revoking those choices prevents hidden continuation.

User-Directed Local Collection. The user chooses when to request audience collection through the extension's interface. Once started, CT Audience Manager may process supported follower, following and account observations automatically through the user's existing X browser session. The user's active choice is recorded locally with the relevant feature scope, version of the displayed information and time of the decision. Users may withdraw applicable collection permissions; withdrawal stops future collection under the revoked scope, but does not automatically erase existing local records.

Permission records and separate technical authentication. After a real Collection or Metrics decision, CT records the decision locally and queues a minimal event for a separate server journal. It creates or refreshes a dedicated anonymous Supabase authentication account for that journal without requesting email, X login, or a Discovery publication. Its random Auth user ID identifies a technical installation account; it does not identify or verify the owner of an X account. Journal credentials are stored in trusted local extension storage and used only for authentication, not included in event rows. Opening the consent dialog alone does not create an event or authenticate this account.

Events contain a UUID and ordering sequence, Collection or Metrics scope, grant/reconfirmation/revocation or separate mode-selection action, exact disclosure version and SHA-256, language, Privacy and Terms version identifiers, extension version and device-reported UTC time. The server assigns its own receipt time and authenticated technical account ID. A private technical-account registry is created on journal signup to keep this identity separate from Discovery, without recording a grant merely on signup. Private last-use and rate-limit counters support the technical account and abuse controls. The journal does not contain X handles or X IDs, audience lists, score values, notes, passwords, cookies, access tokens or browsing history; it has no IP-address column. Server receipt confirms receipt from an authenticated technical account, not real-world identity, X ownership or cryptographic proof that a person clicked a button.

Delivery uses a persistent local queue, ordered retries with the same UUID and server-side deduplication. Temporary unavailability does not prevent local collection or withdrawal. An undelivered event is not shown as server-confirmed. Pending delivery survives normal browser/worker restarts; uninstalling or clearing storage can destroy an undelivered queue and its authentication credentials. Old local-only decisions are not uploaded as fabricated historical grants: the server-specific disclosure requires a new actual confirmation before audit delivery for the new scope.

3. Optional metrics and independent providers

The basic X collection and additional local metric collection have separate controls. Metrics is off by default when you make a new collection choice. If you enable it, the extension may send an X @handle to Moni to retrieve available Moni Score and Moni Smarts. It may also read supported Kaito, Sorsa and XHunt metric badges displayed on X pages by separately installed extensions, and available supported Moni badges. To collect such displayed observations, the extension may scroll relevant X followers/following pages automatically.

The extension does not send your X session cookies or X password to Moni, Kaito, Sorsa, or our Discovery server. Where another extension supplies a badge, that extension has its own data practices. CT Audience Manager cannot control its independent operation.

If you disable or revoke local Metrics, no new local Moni requests or Kaito/Sorsa/XHunt/Moni badge observations are collected. Basic X audience and public-activity collection can continue if Collection remains permitted. Previously stored metric values remain available, unless you separately delete local data. Choosing whether to display a metric is different from granting permission to collect it.

Each contacted service receives the account identifier necessary for the request and ordinary connection data, such as the requesting IP address. When your browser makes the request, it uses your network connection. External avatar hosts likewise receive ordinary image-request information. Unknown or missing metric values are not treated as zero.

Browser-Displayed Observations. When Metrics is enabled, supported Sorsa, XHunt and Kaito score and rank observations can be read from X pages in which corresponding independent browser extensions display them. Those extensions operate independently, and user-initiated automated traversal may cause them to fetch additional observations. CT Audience Manager does not access their private credentials or extension storage for this purpose.

Direct Moni Requests. Moni metrics are handled differently: CT Audience Manager may send supported X account handles directly to Moni to obtain Moni Score and Moni Smarts. These are CT Audience Manager's own requests, not passive readings from another extension's displayed badge.

Separate Discovery Processing. Full locally collected audience lists and metrics maps are not automatically uploaded to our Discovery database. Optional Discovery publication and its independent server-side updates are described separately in Section 4.

Active Metrics selection and withdrawal. Metrics is optional. Badge reading may continue while Metrics is active, even after a scan. Start another scan without Metrics or revoke access in Settings → Privacy to stop new metric collection. An unchecked new scan switches off the active Metrics selection but does not itself revoke an existing grant. A separate mode event may record that selection; it is not a new grant or a full revocation. Repeated starts with unchanged permissions and versions do not create duplicate grants. A user-requested start can update the journal account's last-use time without adding a new grant. A changed disclosure version requires a fresh actual confirmation for the affected scope. Global withdrawal is available in Settings → Privacy and preserves earlier local results.

4. Optional Discovery directory

Discovery is optional and independent of local audience collection. Accessing or refreshing the directory can create an anonymous technical installation account with Supabase, including authentication tokens stored locally in your browser, even before you publish a profile. These credentials are for our directory; they are not your X credentials and do not establish ownership of an X profile.

When you separately agree and choose to publish, the server may store your X handle, display name, avatar URL, visibility status, applicable consent version, available supported observations and their acquisition dates, and public profile counters. Supported observations may include Moni Score, Moni Smarts, Kaito Smarts, and Sorsa Score, where available. Not every metric is provided or refreshed for every profile.

Other directory participants can see a visible profile and its displayed information for matching and filtering. They may copy information they can see. Discovery also processes participant connection events; a participant's indication that they followed someone is not independently verified as an X follow.

For visible published profiles, our server may independently refresh available Moni and Kaito Voices metrics and public X-profile counters through FxEmbed, even while Chrome is closed. This does not upload your full audience or local notes. There is no routine server-side Sorsa lookup. These external services receive the identifiers needed for their queries and the server's connection information.

We maintain a private operational record of Discovery publication and withdrawal actions, including an installation/member identifier, consent version, purpose, action and server time. Profile ownership and participant age are not independently verified. Discovery is intended for adults.

If your own profile appears without your permission, you can use “My profile was published without permission” under Notifications or email us. The form prepares an email in your mail application; you decide whether to send it. A complaint is reviewed manually and does not automatically delete a profile. We may request reasonable evidence that you control the profile, but never your password or session tokens.

7. Retention, hiding and deletion

Local information remains until you delete it through available controls or Chrome storage management, or uninstalling removes the extension's local storage. Stopping or revoking collection does not erase previously saved audience records, metrics, notes, favourites or snapshots. Exported files must be deleted separately by you. Uninstalling the extension does not itself request deletion of an existing server Discovery profile.

Hide profile prevents the directory from displaying your profile and stops its scheduled server refresh, while retaining its server record. Delete profile invokes the supported server-side installation/profile deletion procedure, which requests deletion of the profile, associated observations and connection events, Discovery publication-consent records and related operational records, and its Discovery anonymous installation account. The extension also clears its saved Discovery credentials and cache. The independent Collection/Metrics journal account and your separately stored local audience are unaffected. Hiding a profile deletes neither technical account. If the installation is lost or an older version cannot complete deletion, contact support.

Separate permission journal retention. We retain each scope's latest state/proof while its installation is active; a short technical TTL does not remove the only evidence of a current grant. Superseded history is removed after 90 days by scheduled cleanup. The latest proof of the current state (including revocation) is retained until journal-account deletion. After 365 days without a user-requested collection action or accepted journal event, the journal account is marked inactive and its server permission state expired. After a further 90 days it and related journal records are removed. These inactivity deadlines do not silently revoke the user's local choices or validate old queued events; an expired journal needs deletion and re-creation followed by a new actual choice, or support, before delivery resumes. A separate anonymous Auth account created without a valid event is also subject to this bounded inactivity cleanup. These audit-specific periods are distinct from the Discovery 7/30/90-day operational rules below.

In Settings → Privacy, Delete journal technical account requests deletion of only the journal account and its related events/state. This action also withdraws local Collection and Metrics permission, but preserves saved audience records, notes, favourites, snapshots and unrelated Discovery data. Local historical receipts remain local until local storage is removed. A later new affirmative collection choice can create a new independent journal identity. While offline, deletion remains pending; it is not represented as completed until the server responds. If credentials are lost, an account has expired, a deletion acknowledgement is lost, or the process cannot finish, contact support. Deleting a Discovery profile does not itself delete the separate permission-journal account, and vice versa. Uninstalling does not send either server deletion request.

For a real dispute or binding legal requirement, necessary records may be held under a documented, access-restricted reason and explicit review/end date for no longer than permitted and necessary. A legal hold can defer automatic or requested journal deletion; we explain the applicable restriction when required and review it rather than impose an indefinite default. Requests and applicable rights remain available by email. Backups and provider-controlled operational logs follow their separate applicable retention and deletion processes.

Automated server cleanup is enabled for these existing Discovery technical records:

  • Collection attempts older than 30 days.
  • Completed collection-queue entries older than 7 days.
  • Completed daily collection aggregates older than 90 days, while preserving aggregates needed by pending jobs.

Pending jobs are not removed by these rules. These retention periods do not automatically delete published profiles, profile metric observations or Discovery consent records; those follow the profile deletion or appropriate manual-removal processes. Provider-controlled logs and backups, independently exported files, and copies retained by other people may persist outside our direct control. Emails and records needed to respond to complaints or comply with legal/security duties may be retained for as long as necessary for those purposes, subject to applicable law.

8. Your choices and rights

In Settings → Privacy, you can stop active collection and revoke local Collection or Metrics permission. Revoking Collection stops new X collection and dependent local enrichment. Revoking Metrics stops new local metrics requests and badge collection, but does not prevent basic X collection where Collection remains enabled. Saved jobs respect the latest choices after restart. Closing a consent dialog without choosing is not the same as revoking an existing permission.

Journal delivery can be pending or paused without blocking a local feature choice. You can request deletion through the technical account controls and request a journal copy or assistance by contacting support; journal authentication is separate from Discovery.

Discovery has its own publication consent. Revoking local Collection or Metrics does not automatically hide a published Discovery profile or end its separately authorised server updates. Use Hide profile to stop directory visibility and server refresh, or Delete profile to request deletion.

Depending on the applicable law and circumstances, you may request information or access, correction, deletion, restriction, portability or objection, and may withdraw consent for operations that rely on it. Withdrawal does not invalidate lawful processing already carried out. You may complain to a competent data-protection authority where that right applies. People who have never installed CT Audience Manager may also contact us about an identifiable profile record.

Please send requests to UkrainianVapeMasters@gmail.com. We may need proportionate verification to prevent deletion of someone else's information; never send an X password, cookies, or tokens. We cannot directly delete information stored exclusively on another person's device or in their independent exports.
